## Imagined illustrations

Files ending in `-imagined.jpg` are newly generated editorial-comic interpretations, NOT recovered historical portraits, authenticated likenesses, or reconstructions. At the site owner’s request, no visual AI/question-mark badges are shown; this research record and the prompt manifest retain the distinctions. Prompts are preserved in `imagined-portrait-prompts.json`; generation used the built-in imagegen tool. Images are compressed local JPEGs. Original generated PNGs were preserved outside the published site.

The search considered biography pages, available Commons media, institutional portrait catalogues and variant names. For most illustrated early scholars, no usable, reliably identified portrait was found. Two exceptions concern retrieval rather than absence:

- **Arch Waugh Naylor**: [University of Michigan obituary](https://ece.engin.umich.edu/stories/arch-w-naylor-in-memoriam-1929-2022) explicitly contains a portrait in the search index, but the source repeatedly returned HTTP 403 during download attempts. The illustration is a temporary invented appearance, not evidence that no photograph exists.
- **Alan Breck Macnee**: the author biography in [Proceedings of the IRE, November 1949](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-IRE/40s/IRE-1949-11.pdf) is a promising photograph source; retrieval repeatedly returned HTTP 403. Its image could not be inspected. The illustration is temporary and must not be interpreted as his likeness.

## Johann Pasch: research-area correction

The research-area label inferred from the title *Conjunctiones in genere* was removed. **Theology · Philology / 神学 · 语文学** describes his broader documented work: the [German Digital Library authority record](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/person/gnd/121818284) identifies him as a Lutheran theologian and lists works on Homer and Hebrew terms; the [Rostock professor catalogue](https://cpr.uni-rostock.de/resolve/id/cpr_person_00002533) records his poetry professorship, 1687–1693. The astronomical dissertation remains in the dissertation field, not as a stand-in for his whole research career. These records disagree on the death location and the catalogue gives 1682 for the Magister, versus the genealogy's 1683 dissertation date; those discrepancies should not be silently treated as settled.
